# The Sovereign Day

Time is not a fact. It is a jurisdiction, and you have been living
under someone else's rule. We have the technology to be free, own your day.

1. A day is real. A rhythm, a sleep and a waking.
   Everything smaller or larger than a day is invented.

2. Seconds, minutes, hours: Babylon's base 60, borrowed and never
   questioned. Weeks: a Sabbath cycle wearing the costume of
   astronomy. Months: a moon we stopped looking at, still dictating
   our paperwork. None of it was built for you. All of it was
   inherited.

3. Inherited systems are not neutral. They are old decisions, made by
   people who will never meet you, still governing how you count your
   own life in a digital world.

4. A system with six units has six places to be wrong. A system with
   one unit has one.

5. We do not adjust for the calendar's mistakes. We do not observe
   leap years, leap seconds, or any other patch stitched on to keep a
   broken stack from drifting. Patches are what you need when the
   foundation is not rock solid to begin with.

6. The day is biological, not political. It needs no ratification.

7. Everything else, the week, the month, moonrise, sunrise,
   perihelion all still exists. It is demoted from unit to event: looked
   up, not baked in haphazardly.

8. Time arithmetic should be simple. `.6875 + .3125` is addition.
   `2:45 + 1:30` is a chore. We reject systems that turn sums into
   homework.

9. Four digits are enough for a human life. One tick, 8.64 seconds,
   is smaller than any decision you will make today. This is about 
   personal scheduling, scientists should keep using SI seconds.

10. Midnight is not sacred. It is simply where the old system chose
    to cut you in half. You should cut where you are least likely to 
    be awake instead. Probably around 3AM. But it's your call to make.

11. As the boundary bends to you. So does the length of the day. Earth's
    rotation is the default, not the law; a longer day for a sailor
    keeping their own watch, a shorter one for a body that sleeps
    twice, a Martian sol if you pilot an off-world robot. Again,
    you do you, no need to justify your choice.

12. No day outranks another. Every single day should be planned, 
    executed with real intent, and celebrated, be it for rest or work.
    Weekend is not an excuse for slack, diets shouldn't wait for a 
    new month, resolutions shouldn't occur only at the dawn of a new year.
    You are alive again, claim your day.

13. We do not coordinate. We do not standardize. We do not ask the
    rest of the world to read our clock, and we are not building one
    for them to read.

14. An epoch is not a timezone. It is a moment you chose, frozen,
    carrying no country, no committee, no daylight saving. Set once.
    Moved only when you are moved; and even then, by you, on
    purpose, never automatically, never by a database that thinks it
    knows where you are.

15. Coordination was never the goal. Self-legibility was. Conversion in
and out of your system is out of scope but equally simple for any kind of 
event, natural or human.

16. This is not a proposal. It asks no one's ratification and awaits
    no consensus. A journal does not need a committee to be true, and
    neither does a clock kept for one person.

17. Set your epoch, set your boundary, set your length of day. Then stop negotiating with time, and start keeping it.

"Pluck the day. Trust the next one least of all." - Horace
